Harmony Editor: visual-first mockups

The remote is the canvas. You drag commands, activities and Home Assistant entities onto its keys and its screen, and you see what the remote does in each activity. Tables are a secondary "List view". Desktop frames are 1320 px wide and scroll sideways if your window is narrower. Phone frames are 400 px. The remote is always drawn as the physical (dark) object, and its screen uses the remote's own direction-B look.

1 · Remote studio: assigning hard keys (layer "Stream")

Pick a layer above the remote. Keys this layer overrides glow teal, keys taken from Default carry a small dot, and dimmed keys do nothing. Here the user is dragging vol_up onto Volume + (the drop target pulses and a label previews the result), while the Select key's popover is open with the click-only route: pick from suggestions or search.

2 · X-ray: everything a layer does, at once

The X-ray toggle labels every key with its action, using the same layer colours. It is meant for checking a layer, and it is also what the overview and apply diffs reuse. Hovering a single key shows the same label as a tooltip.

3 · Remote studio: editing the screen

"Screen" zooms the same canvas onto the display at 2×. You edit widgets in place: drag to move, use the corner handles to resize, and a small toolbar floats over the selection. Here a light is being dragged from the Home tab onto a free cell, and the ghost shows the tile it will create (with a toggle action). Look is chosen from rendered swatches. Pages sit in a strip of real thumbnails underneath, and "+" opens the template picker.

4 · Activity: signal chain, timeline, who handles it

Devices are wired left to right, and the pill on each wire is the input the next device switches to (click a pill to change it). You add a device by dropping it on the open slot. The start sequence is a timeline: auto steps are dashed, your own steps can be dragged, and settle delays are drawn as gaps. On the right, you drag a device onto a zone around the mini remote; the keys that zone covers light up.

5 · Devices: gallery and keypad

The gallery shows each device's illustration, model and how its power is controlled. Opening a device shows its commands as a keypad grouped by function. Each key has its own amber Send button: it is a separate target from the key itself, so clicking a key to rename it never sends IR.
